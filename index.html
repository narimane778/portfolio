A little about me

Engineering foundation.
Curiosity beyond it.

My academic background is in chemical and process engineering. During my final-year project at SONATRACH’s Algiers refinery, I studied the valorisation of two petroleum by-products—slurry and bitumen—by converting them into activated carbons through carbonisation and chemical activation using potassium hydroxide (KOH).

I then evaluated the materials for adsorption treatment of refinery effluent, focusing on chemical oxygen demand (COD/DCO). My work included physicochemical characterisation, effluent analysis, calibration and COD measurements, and studying the effects of contact time, pH, adsorbent dose, and initial COD concentration. I also compared adsorption isotherm and kinetic models. Alongside this research, I completed hands-on laboratory quality-control training and am developing my Python and data-analysis skills.

Master’s final-year project · SONATRACH

Valorisation of slurry and bitumen into activated carbon

Studied the conversion of two refinery by-products into activated carbon using carbonisation and KOH chemical activation. The raw materials were characterised using density, viscosity, flash-point, pour-point, and Conradson carbon-residue tests.

Adsorption treatment of refinery effluent

Compared slurry-derived and bitumen-derived activated carbons for COD/DCO removal. Evaluated contact time, pH, adsorbent dose, and initial COD concentration; analysed adsorption isotherms (Langmuir, Freundlich, Sips) and kinetics (pseudo-first-order and pseudo-second-order).

Introductory microbiological analysis

Training exposure to filtration-based analysis of tap water for E. coli and other specified microorganisms, alongside sample preparation, broth and agar use, inoculation, and colony observation.

Microbiology basicsFiltrationCulture media
Selected findings from the Mémoire: The study identified contact times of 20 minutes for slurry-derived carbon and 35 minutes for bitumen-derived carbon, with selected adsorbent doses of 0.02 g and 0.04 g, respectively. The thesis reports that Freundlich best fitted the COD adsorption data (R² = 0.998 for slurry and 0.999 for bitumen), while pseudo-second-order kinetics gave strong fits (R² = 0.997 and 0.999, respectively). Bitumen-derived carbon showed slightly stronger overall performance in the study. These are findings from this specific experimental study, not claims of industrial-scale validation.
